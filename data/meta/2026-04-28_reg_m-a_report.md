# Pokémon Champions Meta Scouting Report — Regulation M-A — 2026-04-28

## Data Provenance

Source: gen9vgc2026regi (623,191 battles)
Raw file: reg_i_chaos.json
Regulation: reg_m-a (155 of 259 Pokemon matched)

> **⚠️ PROXY DATA WARNING:** Stats come from the Regulation I ladder (Scarlet & Violet, gen9vgc2026regi), which permitted restricted legendaries (Miraidon, Calyrex-Ice, Calyrex-Shadow, Kyogre, Koraidon, Urshifu-RS, etc.) that are not legal in Regulation M-A. Teammate correlations are dominated by restricted Pokemon and are unreliable for M-A team building. Usage percentages for Reg M-A legal Pokemon are directionally useful but absolute values are inflated or deflated by the restricted-heavy meta context. **Move and item frequencies on individual Pokemon are the most reliable signal in this dataset.** 104 allowed Pokemon have zero usage data — they are entirely absent from the proxy ladder.
>
> Community data from early Reg M-A tournaments (20 days of results as of this report) has been integrated to supplement and where possible correct for proxy data bias.

## Top Threats

### 1. Incineroar — 42.57% usage
- **Sets:** Intimidate (99.1%), Sitrus Berry (14.3%); Fake Out (98.9%), Knock Off (90.6%), Flare Blitz (69.6%), Parting Shot (69.0%), Will-O-Wisp (16.9%)
- **Strengths:** The universal glue. Fake Out disruption, double Intimidate from Parting Shot, and reliable Dark/Fire coverage. Present on nearly half of all teams. Incineroar is never a surprise — managing around it is the game.
- **Weaknesses:** Ground (x2), Water (x2). Kingambit's Defiant turns every Intimidate into a free +2 Attack. Physical attacker — anything with Intimidate mirror or Competitive/Defiant hard-punishes its support moves.
- **Common partners (proxy):** Whimsicott, Farigiraf, Urshifu-RS (restricted), Calyrex-Shadow (restricted)

### 2. Whimsicott — 15.62% usage
- **Sets:** Prankster (99.2%), Focus Sash (26.2%); Tailwind (97.9%), Moonblast (82.4%), Encore (76.5%), Light Screen (30.4%), Taunt (26.1%), Fake Tears (16.3%)
- **Strengths:** Prankster Tailwind is the format's primary speed control. Prankster Encore (76.5%) locks opponents out of TR setup or Protect sequences. Frail, but Focus Sash guarantees it gets Tailwind up at minimum.
- **Weaknesses:** Fragile — any chip before Sash activation, or spread Rock move, removes it. Dark-types are immune to Prankster-boosted moves, hard-countering its Encore and Taunt. TR completely reverses its game plan.
- **Common partners (proxy):** Miraidon (restricted), Incineroar, Calyrex-Shadow (restricted)

### 3. Farigiraf — 11.48% usage
- **Sets:** Armor Tail (98.4%), Sitrus Berry (10.5%) / Mental Herb (7.9%); Trick Room (93.3%), Helping Hand (65.4%), Foul Play (52.6%), Psychic (48.6%), Protect (34.9%), Imprison (13.8%)
- **Strengths:** The dominant TR setter. Armor Tail blocks priority Fake Out and Sucker Punch on the setup turn, making TR significantly more reliable than any prior setter. Helping Hand applies offensive value every turn after TR. Imprison (13.8%) preemptively locks the opponent's TR.
- **Weaknesses:** Taunt completely shuts it down (no Magic Bounce). Dark-type moves hit its Psychic typing for super-effective damage. Non-priority Taunt before it moves is the cleanest counter.
- **Common partners (proxy):** Iron Hands (restricted), Incineroar, Calyrex-Ice (restricted)

### 4. Volcarona — 3.99% usage
- **Sets:** Flame Body (99.6%), Leftovers (25.2%) / Focus Sash (1.0%); Protect (68.1%), Fiery Dance (59.0%), Quiver Dance (58.3%), Rage Powder (46.4%), Struggle Bug (46.2%), Giga Drain (28.4%)
- **Strengths:** Dual-function threat. Quiver Dance sweeper build on 58% of sets — Leftovers + Fiery Dance boosts itself while dealing damage. Rage Powder redirector on 46% of sets — draws attacks away from the partner while Struggle Bug nukes SpAtk. The Flame Body contact-burn punishes physical attackers.
- **Weaknesses:** Rock x4 — dies to any Rock move before it moves. Water x2. These are both common coverage types.
- **Common partners (proxy):** Incineroar, Grimmsnarl (restricted), Rillaboom (restricted)

### 5. Dragonite — 3.56% usage
- **Sets:** Inner Focus (74.3%) / Multiscale (25.7%), item varies; ExtremeSpeed (91.6%), Outrage (55.5%), Low Kick (49.7%), Aqua Jet (45.3%), Scale Shot (16.3%)
- **Strengths:** Inner Focus prevents Fake Out flinch, letting it act freely turn 1. ExtremeSpeed at +2 priority is the late-game finisher — picks off weakened threats after Tailwind or after speed control is won. Multiscale builds survive one hit at full HP.
- **Weaknesses:** Ice x4 (Multiscale build), Rock x2. Fragile once Multiscale is broken. Needs speed control to function — mediocre without Tailwind or against TR.
- **Common partners (proxy):** Amoonguss (restricted), restricted attackers

### 6. Torkoal — 3.23% usage
- **Sets:** Drought (97.9%), Charcoal (39.3%); Eruption (93.4%), Earth Power (56.3%), Protect (52.6%), Heat Wave (47.6%), Weather Ball (40.6%), Solar Beam (29.0%)
- **Strengths:** The Sun TR engine. Base 20 Speed moves dead last under TR. Charcoal max-HP Eruption under Sun hits both targets for enormous damage. Earth Power covers Rock/Fire-resistant Steel-types. Drought pairs with Chlorophyll, Solar Beam, and Venusaur to form a complete Sun core.
- **Weaknesses:** Water x2, Ground x2, Rock x2 — three common coverage types. HP depletion tanks Eruption's power fast. Any weather override kills its damage output immediately.
- **Common partners (proxy):** Calyrex-Ice (restricted), Indeedee-F, Hatterene

### 7. Archaludon — 3.02% usage
- **Sets:** Stamina (85.4%), Leftovers (6.8%); Electro Shot (94.5%), Flash Cannon (76.1%), Body Press (66.7%), Draco Meteor (52.6%), Dragon Pulse (29.7%), Protect (23.6%)
- **Strengths:** The Rain team's offensive anchor. Electro Shot charges in one turn under rain for 130 BP with a SpAtk boost. Stamina + Body Press creates a secondary damage axis — every physical hit makes Body Press hit harder. Ground is the only clean defensive answer.
- **Weaknesses:** Ground x2 (absolute), Fighting x2. Loses all offensive value if rain is cleared. Raichu's Lightning Rod absorbs Electro Shot on rain mirrors, but Discharge bypasses redirection.
- **Common partners (proxy):** Pelipper, Basculegion, Raichu

### 8. Pelipper — 2.30% usage
- **Sets:** Drizzle (98.5%), Focus Sash (38.8%); Hurricane (90.6%), Weather Ball (69.3%), Wide Guard (68.7%), Tailwind (59.4%), Protect (48.0%)
- **Strengths:** Non-restricted rain setter. Focus Sash guarantees rain goes up. Wide Guard (68.7%) blocks spread moves — Eruption, Blizzard, Discharge — for both teammates. Tailwind gives it dual speed/weather control in one slot.
- **Weaknesses:** Rock x4. Electric x2. Extremely frail without Sash intact — chip damage before the game ends Pelipper's utility entirely.
- **Common partners:** Archaludon, Basculegion, Raichu

### 9. Sinistcha — 2.20% usage
- **Sets:** Hospitality (96.4%), Sitrus Berry (20.6%) / Focus Sash (18.7%); Rage Powder (86.8%), Matcha Gotcha (82.3%), Trick Room (45.9%), Life Dew (43.1%), Shadow Ball (39.4%), Protect (37.0%)
- **Strengths:** The format's primary Rage Powder redirector without Amoonguss in the pool. Hospitality heals the partner on switch-in — excellent passive recovery for rotating TR teams. Dual-function: roughly half the sets also set TR (45.9%). Ghost typing makes it completely immune to Fake Out.
- **Weaknesses:** Dark x2. No offensive presence — pure support. Frail enough that targeted Dark moves remove it before it redirects.
- **Common partners (proxy):** Incineroar, Terapagos (restricted)

### 10. Ninetales-Alola — 2.19% usage
- **Sets:** Snow Warning (97.9%), Focus Sash (17.3%); Aurora Veil (96.5%), Blizzard (66.3%), Protect (50.9%), Moonblast (49.3%), Icy Wind (26.8%), Encore (24.0%)
- **Strengths:** Aurora Veil halves damage from all moves (physical and special) for 8 turns under Hail/Snow. Focus Sash guarantees Veil goes up. Icy Wind provides speed control on the same turn. Encore disrupts follow-up setup.
- **Weaknesses:** Rock x2, Steel x2, Fire x2. Frail — Sash-reliant. Tyranitar is specifically rising in the meta to replace Snow and counter Mega Froslass (see community section).
- **Common partners (proxy):** Incineroar, restricted attackers

### 11. Kingambit — 2.02% usage
- **Sets:** Defiant (86.9%), Black Glasses (27.0%); Sucker Punch (90.1%), Kowtow Cleave (89.4%), Iron Head (86.4%), Protect (46.7%), Low Kick (28.8%), Swords Dance (20.0%)
- **Strengths:** Defiant turns every Intimidate into +2 Attack — and Incineroar is on 42% of all teams. Black Glasses Sucker Punch + Kowtow Cleave is priority-adjacent damage every turn. Fighting x4 weakness, but Kingambit with even one Swords Dance becomes unkillable through conventional means.
- **Weaknesses:** Fighting x4 — the clearest hard counter in the format. Fire x2, Ground x2.
- **Common partners (proxy):** Incineroar, Flutter Mane (restricted)

### 12. Ditto — 1.88% usage
- **Sets:** Imposter (98.7%), Choice Scarf (46.5%) / Focus Sash (28.3%); Transform (100%)
- **Sets (two distinct builds):** Scarf Ditto outspeeds and copies the fastest opposing threat. Focus Sash Ditto survives a TR attacker's hit and copies it to use its own power against the TR team.
- **Strengths:** Scarf Ditto is the universal speed check — it copies any Pokemon and outspeeds it. Sash Ditto is a dedicated TR counter that mirrors the slow attacker. The item choice completely determines its role.
- **Weaknesses:** Cannot choose which Pokemon to copy — gets the leading opponent. Must be on the correct side of the field to copy the right target.
- **Common partners (proxy):** Restricted attackers as copy targets

### 13. Basculegion — 1.80% usage
- **Sets:** Swift Swim (86.4%), item varies; Last Respects (95.0%), Wave Crash (84.9%), Aqua Jet (75.1%), Protect (53.0%), Flip Turn (39.2%)
- **Strengths:** Swift Swim doubles Speed in rain — pairs with Pelipper in M-A. Last Respects gains 50 BP per fainted teammate, making it a late-game nuke that becomes near-unresistable as the game progresses. Community sentiment (Smogon user pengu) advocates S-tier; tournament win at Grand Champions Festival confirms viability.
- **Weaknesses:** Electric x2. Grass x2. Dependent on rain staying up — Tyranitar ends the party.
- **Common partners:** Pelipper, Archaludon

### 14. Sneasler — 1.80% usage
- **Sets:** Unburden (87.8%), Focus Sash (10.6%) / White Herb (6.2%); Dire Claw (91.0%), Close Combat (68.9%), Fake Out (52.8%), Protect (51.8%), Throat Chop (35.3%)
- **Strengths:** Unburden doubles Speed after item consumption — White Herb or Focus Sash activation hits ~240 effective Speed. Dire Claw (91%) has a 50% chance to inflict paralysis, sleep, or poison every turn. Fake Out disruption. Format's highest-usage Pokemon on Showdown's actual M-A ladder (42.7% per Showdown Tier).
- **Weaknesses:** Ground x2, Fire x2, Fighting x2. Floette-Eternal (48.5% Sneasler win rate) and Basculegion hard-counter it per Showdown Tier data.
- **Common partners (proxy):** Restricted attackers

### 15. Sableye — 1.74% usage
- **Sets:** Prankster (98.8%), Focus Sash (20.1%) / Mental Herb (5.3%); Encore (52.6%), Fake Out (37.0%), Foul Play (36.7%), Disable (36.6%), Gravity (29.3%), Quash (29.0%)
- **Strengths:** Prankster Encore punishes any setup or TR. Prankster Disable removes the opponent's last used move. Quash forces the target to act last — a dedicated TR-disruption tool. Ghost/Dark typing is immune to Normal and Fighting.
- **Weaknesses:** Fairy x2. Extremely frail — Focus Sash or Mental Herb are required to survive anything. Cannot damage most of the meta offensively.
- **Common partners (proxy):** Groudon (restricted), restricted attackers

### 16. Raichu — 1.40% usage
- **Sets:** Lightning Rod (98.1%), Focus Sash (39.2%); Fake Out (94.5%), Nuzzle (68.3%), Volt Switch (54.5%), Endeavor (37.2%), Electroweb (19.5%)
- **Strengths:** Lightning Rod absorbs all single-target Electric moves aimed at Rain-core teammates. Nuzzle paralyzes while dealing chip. Volt Switch provides pivot momentum. Sash + Endeavor applies massive chip at low HP. Specific to Rain builds.
- **Weaknesses:** Ground x2. Frail — pure support with no offensive presence without Endeavor. Useless if the rain Pokemon it protects are already gone.
- **Common partners:** Pelipper, Archaludon

### 17. Maushold — 1.37% usage
- **Sets:** Friend Guard (73.4%) / Technician (25.9%), Focus Sash (22.9%); Follow Me (84.2%), Population Bomb (48.1%), Helping Hand (47.7%), Taunt (42.2%), Protect (41.3%), Feint (31.0%)
- **Strengths:** Follow Me is the second Rage Powder option in the format (with Sinistcha). Friend Guard cuts partner damage by 25%. Taunt (42.2%) shuts down opposing support on the same turn. Feint (31.0%) removes Protect before a big attack lands.
- **Weaknesses:** Fighting x2. Frail. Taunt from a Dark-type is immune to the Prankster it doesn't have — no priority disruption. Mold Breaker Tinkaton can Fake Out through the Friend Guard/Follow Me combination.
- **Common partners (proxy):** Calyrex-Shadow (restricted), Zamazenta-Crowned (restricted)

### 18. Tyranitar — 1.24% usage
- **Sets:** Sand Stream (98.4%), Leftovers (5.0%) / Sitrus Berry (3.8%); Rock Slide (77.9%), Knock Off (68.0%), Protect (32.0%), Low Kick (21.7%), Crunch (19.2%)
- **Strengths:** Sand Stream is the weather-removal tool — specifically rising to counter Snow (Mega Froslass) and Rain. Rock Slide as spread damage hits the format's many Flying- and Fire-types. Knock Off removes Focus Sashes and support items, crippling Whimsicott and Pelipper.
- **Weaknesses:** Fighting x4, Ground x2, Water x2, Grass x2. Slow at base 61 Speed.
- **Common partners (proxy):** Excadrill

### 19. Hatterene — 0.97% usage
- **Sets:** Magic Bounce (90.7%), Focus Sash (10.9%) / Leftovers (6.8%); Dazzling Gleam (83.5%), Trick Room (81.8%), Expanding Force (68.1%), Protect (41.5%), Psychic (29.9%), Mystical Fire (21.4%)
- **Strengths:** Magic Bounce reflects Taunt back at the user, protecting TR setup. Expanding Force with Indeedee-F's Psychic Surge hits both opponents at 1.5x power — spread Psychic nukes. The Hatterene + Indeedee-F TR core (68.8% teammate rate) is the most reliable Psychic Terrain TR shell.
- **Weaknesses:** Poison x2, Steel x2, Ghost x2. Frail despite the Sash. Only 29 base Speed means it sets TR safely but is otherwise the slowest non-abuser on the field.
- **Common partners:** Indeedee-F, Torkoal

### 20. Araquanid — 0.84% usage
- **Sets:** Water Bubble (96.6%), Mystic Water (24.9%) / Leftovers (11.3%); Liquidation (86.9%), Wide Guard (77.7%), Protect (51.9%), Leech Life (41.5%), Infestation (19.9%)
- **Strengths:** Water Bubble doubles Water move power AND halves Fire damage received. Mystic Water Liquidation effectively hits as hard as a Choice Band Water move. Wide Guard (77.7%) blocks opposing spread moves — Eruption, Blizzard, Discharge. Base 42 Speed moves last in TR. Legitimate TR attacker and spread blocker in one slot.
- **Weaknesses:** Electric x2, Grass x2, Flying x2. No priority. Requires TR to be set to threaten offensive damage.
- **Common partners (proxy):** Indeedee-F, restricted attackers

## Core Combinations

### Core 1: Whimsicott + [Restricted Attacker] — Tailwind HO Shell
The format's most common core structure (proxy data). Whimsicott provides priority Tailwind and Encore, the restricted attacker (Miraidon in proxy, replaced by the best available legal attacker in M-A) does the damage. In M-A, this shell runs with Sneasler, Basculegion, Kingambit, or Dragonite as the secondary attacker. Incineroar is the universal third piece — Fake Out + Intimidate + Parting Shot supports whichever two attackers lead.

### Core 2: Farigiraf + [Slow Attacker] — Standard Trick Room
Farigiraf + Iron Hands (restricted proxy) → in M-A, Farigiraf pairs with Torkoal, Araquanid, Kingambit, or Hatterene as the TR attackers. Incineroar again as the support pivot. Armor Tail makes the setup turn reliable. Helping Hand after TR is up maximizes the attacker's damage output. The cleanest legal TR spine in the format.

### Core 3: Pelipper + Archaludon — Rain Offense
Pelipper Drizzle → Archaludon one-turn Electro Shot. Wide Guard from either covers opposing spread. Raichu provides Lightning Rod umbrella and Nuzzle speed control. Basculegion as the Swift Swim sweeper that takes over when Archaludon is checked. Strongest non-TR offensive core in the format.

### Core 4: Hatterene + Indeedee-F — Psychic Terrain TR
Hatterene + Indeedee-F (68.8% teammate rate in data). Indeedee-F's Psychic Surge blocks priority moves before TR is established — a different protection layer than Armor Tail. Indeedee-F's Follow Me redirects. Torkoal as the damage engine under TR + Sun. The Expanding Force nuke under Psychic Terrain (1.5x boosted spread Psychic) is a wincon many teams are not prepared for.

### Core 5: Torkoal + Venusaur — Sun Offense
Outside TR: Torkoal Drought enables Venusaur Chlorophyll (Speed doubles to 160) and makes Sleep Powder 100% accurate (Sleep Clause still applies). With Safety Goggles absent from the item pool, Sleep Powder is significantly more threatening than in previous formats. Venusaur + Petal Blizzard + Sludge Bomb provides spread damage under sun while Torkoal Erupts. Early community alert (Smogon) about this interaction — underinternalized by the field as of this report.

### Core 6: Ninetales-Alola + [Physical Wall] — Aurora Veil Setup
Ninetales-Alola sets Aurora Veil under Snow (halves all damage for 8 turns), then pivots out. A slow, physical wall (Araquanid, Garganacl) tanks under Veil while threatening back. The core does not require a restricted attacker — it's a defensive shell that delays the opponent's win condition while building advantage through durability. Tyranitar counters this core by replacing Snow with Sand.

## Dominant Archetypes

### Archetype 1: Miraidon Tailwind Hyper-Offense (~15–20% of teams in proxy, adjusted lower in M-A)
- **Core Pokemon:** [Best legal fast attacker], Whimsicott, Incineroar, + 3 flex (Sneasler, Kingambit, Dragonite, Basculegion)
- **Win condition:** Prankster Tailwind turn 1, then sweep for 4 turns. Incineroar Fake Out prevents the opponent from acting turn 1. Encore locks any Protect or TR attempt.
- **Strengths:** Fastest clock in the format. Beats TR if Tailwind lands before TR does. No weather dependency.
- **Weaknesses:** TR completely reverses the game plan. Taunt on Whimsicott (non-priority from a Dark-type or before Prankster fires) denies Tailwind entirely. Fragile once Whimsicott is gone.

### Archetype 2: Farigiraf Trick Room (~8–12% of teams)
- **Core Pokemon:** Farigiraf (TR setter), Torkoal / Araquanid / Kingambit (TR attackers), Incineroar (Fake Out), Sinistcha (backup TR + Rage Powder)
- **Win condition:** Armor Tail setup → TR lands clean → slow attackers obliterate under reversed speed. Parting Shot cycling on Incineroar controls which attacker gets in at the right moment.
- **Strengths:** Most reliable TR setup in the format. Helping Hand maximizes damage. Excellent into Tailwind HO. Incineroar mirror neutralizes the opponent's physical damage.
- **Weaknesses:** Taunt (non-priority) shuts Farigiraf. Imprison as a mirror — Farigiraf itself runs it on 13.8% of sets. Rain archetype does not care about TR speed advantage at 90 base Speed Kyogre equivalent.

### Archetype 3: Rain Offense (~5–8% of teams)
- **Core Pokemon:** Pelipper (Drizzle), Archaludon (Electro Shot), Basculegion (Swift Swim + Last Respects), Raichu (Lightning Rod + Nuzzle), Incineroar (support)
- **Win condition:** Rain goes up, Archaludon charges Electro Shot free every turn, Basculegion sweeps in the mid/late game with escalating Last Respects power.
- **Strengths:** No single answer covers all three win conditions. Wide Guard blocks opposing spread. Raichu provides both Electric immunity and speed control. Basculegion's Last Respects late-game scaling is effectively uncounterable without specific preparation.
- **Weaknesses:** Tyranitar replaces Rain with Sand. TR at 90 base Speed equivalents still means key pieces move mid-speed-tier under TR — Archaludon at 85 base is not slow. Ground coverage eliminates Archaludon.

### Archetype 4: Sun Trick Room (~3–4% of teams)
- **Core Pokemon:** Torkoal (Drought), Hatterene or Oranguru (TR setter), Indeedee-F (Psychic Surge + Follow Me), Calyrex-Ice equivalent or Ursaluna (TR attacker)
- **Win condition:** Psychic Surge blocks priority on setup turn, Hatterene/Oranguru sets TR, Torkoal Eruption + TR attacker double-hits under reversed speed.
- **Strengths:** Most oppressive TR shell in the format when it goes off. Indeedee-F's Psychic Surge is priority-blocking protection orthogonal to Armor Tail. Expanding Force nuke is a surprise win condition.
- **Weaknesses:** Weather war — Drizzle, Grassy Terrain, or Sand flips the sun. Hatterene is frail before Indeedee-F gets set up. Extremely predictable bring pattern; most teams know how to approach it.

### Archetype 5: Calyrex-Shadow Balance / Semi-Room (~8–12% in proxy)
- **Core Pokemon:** [Best available special attacker], Maushold or Sinistcha (Follow Me / Rage Powder), Incineroar (Fake Out), Dragonite (ExtremeSpeed cleanup)
- **Win condition:** Redirection absorbs targeting on the primary attacker. Dragonite ExtremeSpeed finishes weakened threats. Flexible secondary slot for coverage.
- **Strengths:** Goes even into most archetypes. Maushold Taunt shuts Whimsicott and Farigiraf simultaneously. Flexible restricted equivalent slot.
- **Weaknesses:** Kingambit Defiant fires on Parting Shot. Needs redirection support to avoid getting blown up immediately.

### Archetype 6: Koraidon Sun Offense (~5–7% in proxy)
- **Core Pokemon:** [Sun-boosted physical attacker], Incineroar, Talonflame or Whimsicott (Tailwind), Flutter Mane equivalent or Rillaboom equivalent
- **Win condition:** Drought + Tailwind + boosted physical attacker. Grassy Terrain (Rillaboom proxy) blocks opposing TR.
- **Strengths:** Does not need dedicated weather setter if running Drought ability. Fast clock under Tailwind. Grassy Terrain is the primary TR prevention tool on these builds.
- **Weaknesses:** Rain flips the sun. Loses to TR because the win condition relies on outspeeding. Calyrex-Ice equivalent doesn't care about speed tiers.

### Archetype 7: Grassy Terrain Balance / Goodstuffs (~10–15%)
- **Core Pokemon:** [Terrain setter], Incineroar, flexible attacker, + whatever the team needs
- **Win condition:** Grassy Terrain denies TR (blocks Spore, reduces Ground damage), priority Grassy Glide for chip cleanup. No single win condition — exploits opponent's overcommitment.
- **Strengths:** Hard to prepare against. Flexible into most matchups. Grassy Terrain specifically answers Sun TR (blocks Spore enabler) and Sand (replaces weather).
- **Weaknesses:** No inherent speed control. Leans heavily on the primary attacker's raw power.

**Archetype Matchup Summary:**
- Rain beats TR. Fast teams move before TR is set at base 90 Speed equivalents, and Ground coverage dispatches TR attackers.
- Tailwind HO beats most archetypes but can be Taunted or reversed by TR if the setter survives.
- TR beats Tailwind HO if TR lands before Tailwind doubles — Armor Tail makes this more likely.
- Balance/Goodstuffs goes reasonably into everything — it's the "answer the opponent" style.
- Every top archetype can beat every other archetype with the right bring-4 decision. The format rewards lead selection over raw power.

## Exploitable Weaknesses

### 1. Ground Coverage Hits 8 of the Top 24 Threats
Ground hits Incineroar, Torkoal, Archaludon, Kingambit, Sneasler, Raichu, Tyranitar, and Armarouge for super-effective damage. Incineroar alone is on 42% of teams. Earthquake as a spread move hits the majority of any given opposing team in this meta. The primary immunity in the top 20 is Flying-types (Dragonite, Pelipper, Talonflame) — which are setup pieces, not win conditions. The win conditions are Ground-weak.

**Exploit:** Earthquake cleanup through the field. Ghost/Flying-type TR attackers (Golurk-Mega, Runerigus) dodge Ground mirrors while threatening it back.

### 2. Rock Coverage Kills Speed Control Setters
Rock hits 9 of top 24 threats. Volcarona (x4), Talonflame (x4), Dragonite (x2), Pelipper (x2), Ninetales-Alola (x2). Both primary Tailwind setters (Talonflame x4, Volcarona as dual threat) die in one Rock move. Pelipper at x4 dies before it can even set rain if chip damage was applied. Stone Edge on a high-Attack Ground-type covers both weakness clusters simultaneously.

**Exploit:** Lead with a Rock-type or Rock coverage move. Eliminates the opponent's speed control engine before the game starts.

### 3. Incineroar Is a Single Point of Failure for Intimidate
42% of teams run one Intimidate user. Any Pokemon with Defiant, Competitive, or Mirror Armor turns Incineroar's Parting Shot into a free +2 stat boost. Defiant users legal in M-A: Kingambit (already top 20), Passimian (0% usage). Competitive: Milotic (0% usage). Mirror Armor: Corviknight (0% usage). ### 4. Ghost-Type Leads Dodge the Entire Fake Out Sequence
Incineroar runs Fake Out 99% of the time. Sneasler 53%, Raichu 95%. Ghost-types are completely immune. Sinistcha as TR setter + Rage Powder redirector means the opponent's Fake Out does nothing on turn 1 while TR is set safely. **Exploit:** Ghost-type TR setter (Sinistcha, Hatterene with Magic Bounce) + Ghost-type attacker (Golurk-Mega) negates the entire Fake Out + Intimidate sequence.

### 5. Tailwind Is Fragile — TR Reversal Is the Cleanest Counter
Whimsicott at 116 base Speed becomes the *slowest* Pokemon on the field under TR. The Prankster Tailwind it provides is its team's entire speed control. Without Tailwind, offensive teams relying on fast restricted Pokemon have no clock. TR teams that can set TR before Tailwind doubles — or eliminate Whimsicott the turn TR is set — win the speed control war immediately. Encore after Tailwind forces Whimsicott off its support moves.

**Exploit:** Ghost-type TR setter dodges Fake Out → TR is set before Tailwind doubles → Whimsicott is now the slowest thing on the field.

### 6. Focus Sash Over-Reliance Creates Chip Damage Vulnerability
Whimsicott (26.2%), Pelipper (38.8%), Raichu (39.2%), Maushold (22.9%), Sableye (20.1%) all run Focus Sash. These are the format's support pieces. Any prior chip damage (Sandstorm, Hail, Grassy Terrain damage from a partner, or any prior hit) removes Sash and makes them one-shotable. Spread moves that hit both support Pokemon simultaneously break Sash on one and potentially KO the other.

**Exploit:** Apply chip before the game's decisive turns. Rock Slide on turn 1 breaks Pelipper's Sash and pressures Incineroar simultaneously.

### 7. Venusaur Sleep Powder Is Underrated in the Absence of Safety Goggles
Safety Goggles is not in the M-A legal items pool. No item blocks sleep in this format. Venusaur's Sleep Powder under Sun (100% accuracy with Chlorophyll active) is free turn-1 sleep against almost any target. The community (Smogon user pengu) flagged this explicitly — the broader field has not fully internalized the implication. The only counterplay is speed control that outspeeds Venusaur (Tailwind teams) or Lum Berry.

**Exploit:** Venusaur in a Sun core is an under-prepared threat. Conversely, Lum Berry is critical on any lead Pokemon against Sun teams.
